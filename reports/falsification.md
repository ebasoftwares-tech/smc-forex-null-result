## What this report does NOT establish

conclusions below.** Every arm is measured on real FX majors over
40 symbol-years, so a null here is the *strategy* speaking rather
than the fixture -- which is the opposite of every previous run of this suite.

## The declared equivalence margin

**0.10 R**, fixed before any arm was run, and not chosen
freely: it is section 10.1's own expectancy threshold for trading this system live.
A difference smaller than the number the project already committed to as the
boundary of a tradable edge cannot be a difference that matters.

The verdict is **three-way**, never "the CI spans zero, so they are the same":

| Verdict | Condition | What it licenses |
|---|---|---|
| `DIFFERENT` | CI excludes zero | The component contributes |
| `EQUIVALENT` | CI lies entirely inside the margin | **Only this** licenses "contributes nothing" |
| `UNDERPOWERED` | CI spans zero and extends past the margin | The study cannot answer |

H3 and H4 are falsified by a *negative*, so a wide interval around zero is absence
of evidence, not evidence of absence (D-010 section 2).

## Findings

### 1. Section 10.1's falsification row can be cleared on stop width alone

**This is the study's main output and it is a problem with the acceptance criterion,
not with any arm.**

The baseline beats `sweep_only` by +0.064 R per setup net, CI
[0.048, 0.081], and by +0.025 gross. This
arm clears the row in both currencies. The point of this section is the arms
that clear it in **only one**, because the difference between the two columns is
not signal:

| | baseline | `sweep_only` |
|---|---:|---:|
| median stop (ATR) | 2.20 | 0.88 |
| E/setup, **gross** R | +0.003 | -0.021 |
| cost, in R | +0.004 | +0.044 |
| E/setup, **net** R | -0.001 | -0.065 |

The gross delta is +0.025 R with a CI of [0.008, 0.040] -- **it contains zero, which is the correct answer**. The
whole of the net-R gap is transaction cost. `sweep_only` enters at the sweep
confirmation, so its stop sits just beyond an extreme a bar or two old, while the
baseline waits for a CHoCH and its stop sits beyond an extreme up to twelve bars
back. A fixed spread and commission against a stop half as wide is **twice the cost
per R** -- and in net R that is indistinguishable from signal.

The consequence generalises past this fixture:

> **Any control that enters earlier than the baseline has a tighter stop, and
> therefore loses more of its R to costs. Section 10.1's row -- *"beats every
> control by a margin whose CI excludes zero"* -- is stated in expectancy, which is
> net R, so it can be satisfied by geometry rather than by signal.**

**Three arms are affected, not one.** Every arm whose median stop is materially
tighter than the baseline's carries the same inflation in its net delta; only
`sweep_only` inflates far enough to cross zero:

| Arm | median SL (ATR) | net delta | gross delta | inflation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `shuffled_liquidity` | 2.12 | 0.004 | 0.003 | 0.001 |
| `sweep_only` | 0.88 | 0.064 | 0.025 | 0.040 |
| `choch_only` | 2.14 | -0.013 | -0.016 | 0.004 |
| `reversed_order` | 0.85 | 0.063 | 0.024 | 0.039 |
| `random_time` | 0.95 | 0.047 | 0.013 | 0.033 |

`random_time` matters most in that list, because it is the **floor** and the floor
is the row that has to be cleared before any other row means anything. Its stop is
0.95 ATR against the baseline's
2.20, so a baseline that beats random entry
in net R has not thereby shown it has a signal -- it may only have shown that it
waits longer before committing. `choch_only` and `shuffled_liquidity` are clean:
both keep the baseline's stop width, and both agree in the two currencies.

**What to do about it is a decision, not a fix, and it is not taken here** (section 10.2). The options:

1. Read the row in **gross R**, making it a test of signal and losing the point that
   a strategy has to pay its costs to be worth trading.
2. Keep net R and **report both**, treating a net-only win as not demonstrating the
   sequence -- which is what this report does.
3. Match the stop distance across arms, which changes what the controls are: a
   sweep-only arm with the baseline's stop is not "enter on sweep confirmation".

Option 2 is implemented; 1 and 3 are pre-registration decisions and belong in the
pre-registration (section 1), before real bars arrive.

### 2. The shipped default entry model cannot run half the section 6.4 suite

Covered above. The consequence is that section 10.1's most important row is
undefined at `entry.model = C`, and the suite has to name the model it runs at.

### 3. `choch_only` must not be built on `structure.py`'s CHoCH events

The obvious construction is wrong and its failure mode is invisible. A structure
`CHOCH` is a trend flip through the **protected** level; SPEC 11.2's CHoCH -- the
one the baseline trades -- is a break of the **last unbroken swing**. Building the
arm on the former makes it differ from the baseline in the definition of the thing
under test, and its inevitable null then reads as *"the sweep requirement only
reduces sample size"* when what was measured was a stricter break rule. The arm
reuses `MssEngine._major_reference` itself for that reason.

The two counts are close enough that a size check does not catch the error: on one
fixture year the structure events outnumber the correct population and on another
they do not, so the test asserts on *which bars fire* instead.

### 4. Three guards nothing in the fixture reaches -- the pattern, for the third time

D-014 section 8 and D-015 both recorded a rule enforced somewhere no test goes.
Three of the first eighteen mutations here survived for the same reason:

| Guard | Why the fixture never reaches it |
|---|---|
| `placeholder_sweep`'s `trigger_bar` in the id key | No two legs in the fixture happen to share an extreme, so no collision occurs -- but `choch_only` scans every bar and two references broken three bars apart can share one |
| `_leg_extreme`'s direction | Inverting it still produces trades and still reports a null, so only a direct test sees it |
| `choch.max_reference_distance_atr` in `choch_only` | At the FROZEN default of 3.0 it rejects **nothing** -- the widest reference on this fixture sits at about 2.8 ATR |

The third is worth separating from the other two: it is a **measurement, not an
arithmetic impossibility** like D-014's four unreachable defaults. It is an ABLATION
parameter over {2.0, 3.0, 4.0}, and **at 2.0 it binds hard** -- which is how the
branch is now tested. It also echoes STATE.md section 3: the Phase 9 gate is not
robust to this same parameter, and here it is again sitting just past where the data
reaches.

### 5. Three asymmetries between the arms that no construction can remove

| | What | Which way it cuts |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `choch_only` and `random_time` have no sweep, so their `liq_*` columns and `penetration_atr` are placeholders (NaN, tier 0) | No liquidity breakdown is valid over those two arms |
| 2 | The leg origin is **searched** in the sweepless arms and **clamped** to the sweep extreme in the baseline (D-009 section 11) | **Favours the control** -- a searched origin can only displace at least as much |
| 3 | Reversing the order moves the stop anchor onto the event being entered on | Unavoidable: the trigger and the anchor are the same two events |

### 6. What is still not built

An **end-to-end positive control** -- an injected edge surviving the whole chain from
prices to a `DIFFERENT` verdict. The positive control here covers the comparison
layer, and the per-arm tests cover each construction, but nothing demonstrates that a
real conditional edge in the *price series* would come out the other end. Building
one needs a synthetic market with a genuine SMC edge, which is the fixture this
project does not have and cannot easily fabricate -- injecting drift after each MSS
changes the prices, which changes the sweeps, which changes the MSS set.

Sections 6.1 (H2) and 6.2 (H5) are already built as `sweep_study.py` and
`marginal_value.py`. Section 6.5's ablation matrix is not, and is the natural next
piece.
